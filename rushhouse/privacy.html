Rushhouse Diner — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2 September 2026
The short version. Rushhouse Diner has no account, no sign-in and no server of ours. Your progress is saved on your phone and nowhere else, and the developer receives no data from your device at all. Google’s ad service does contact its own servers while the game is open — it asks for your ad-consent choice and fetches a video in advance so that it is ready if you tap to watch one. You are never shown an ad you did not ask for: there are no banners, no interstitials, and nothing that plays by itself. Cooking together with a friend sends the shift between your two phones, and only while you have a session open.

What we collect

Nothing. The developer operates no servers, no analytics, and no crash-reporting service, and receives no data from your device. We could not identify a single player if we tried. That is true whether or not you use co-op: the relay described below is Unity’s, not ours, and we have no account with which to read anything that passes through it.

Cooking together (co-op)

Co-op lets two phones play the same shift. It is off unless you open it, and nothing below happens until you tap CO-OP and either open a kitchen or enter a friend’s code. If you never do, this whole section does not apply to you.

Two phones on mobile networks cannot reach each other directly, so the game uses Unity Relay, a service run by Unity Technologies, to pass messages between them. When you start or join a co-op game:

  • An anonymous player ID is created for you by Unity and stored on your phone. It is a random string. It is not linked to your name, your email, your Apple ID or any account — we never ask you for any of those, and the game has no sign-in screen.
  • Your IP address reaches Unity, as it must for any connection, and is used to pick a nearby relay server.
  • The game state itself — where the chefs are, what is on the counters, the clock — passes through the relay so the other phone can draw it. It is gameplay only; the game has no chat, no voice, no names and no way to type anything except a six-character join code.
  • Unity’s own diagnostics may report errors from its networking code to Unity.

Unity is the data controller for all of that and describes it in the Unity Gaming Services player privacy policy. To stop it entirely, do not use co-op; to remove the anonymous ID from your phone, delete the app.

What is stored on your device

The game keeps one save file locally, under the key rushhouse-unity-save. It holds:

  • your restaurant layout, day number, cash and star ratings
  • perks and upgrades you have unlocked
  • wardrobe tokens, plus which outfits you own and have equipped
  • your settings (music, sound effects, controls)

This never leaves your phone. Deleting the app erases it permanently — we cannot restore it, because we never had a copy.

Rewarded ads, and only rewarded ads

Ads are supplied by Google AdMob. They appear only where you choose to tap a "watch a video" button — for a second chance at a failed day, bonus cash, or wardrobe tokens. You can play the entire game and never watch one.

When you do choose to watch, Google — not us — receives information from your device in order to serve and measure that ad. Google describes this in its own Privacy & Terms, and it typically includes:

DataWhyWho receives it
Device advertising identifier and device information Serving the ad, capping how often it repeats, detecting fraud Google
IP address Delivering the video, and choosing an ad for the right country Google
Ad interaction (that it was shown, watched, or closed) Measuring the ad and paying the developer Google

None of this is linked to a name, email, or account, because the game has none to link it to.

No tracking prompt on iOS

Rushhouse Diner does not ask for permission to track you across other companies' apps and websites, and does not use Apple's advertising identifier (IDFA) for that purpose. You will never see the "Allow app to track your activity?" dialog, because the app never requests it. On iOS this means the ads you see are non-personalised.

Consent in Europe, the UK and Switzerland

If you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, the first launch shows a consent form from Google's User Messaging Platform, asking whether ad partners may use your data. Your answer is honoured — decline and you get non-personalised ads, or none at all.

You can change your mind at any time: open Settings inside the game and choose Privacy options. The same form reopens.

Children

Rushhouse Diner is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children, and — since we collect nothing from anyone — there is nothing for a parent to request the deletion of. Co-op does not change that for us: the anonymous ID and the relay described above belong to Unity, the game asks for no personal detail to use them, and there is no chat, no voice and no way for players to send each other anything but a six-character join code and the movements of a cartoon chef. A parent who would rather their child not connect at all can simply not use co-op. If you have a concern, write to us at the address below.

Your rights

Rights under the GDPR, UK GDPR and CCPA — access, correction, deletion, and opting out of the sale or sharing of personal information — apply to data a company holds about you. We hold none. For the data Google processes when you watch an ad, use Google My Ad Center and your device's ad settings:

  • iOS: Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking, and → Apple Advertising
  • Android: Settings → Google → Ads → Delete advertising ID

To erase everything the game itself stores, delete the app.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. A change that affects what leaves your device will be announced in the app before it takes effect.
