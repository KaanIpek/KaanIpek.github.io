The short version. Shrinefall has no accounts, no shop and no message box. Played by yourself it touches no network whatsoever. The one moment anything leaves the handset is when you open or enter a co-op room, and the sections below set out precisely what that is.

Who stands behind this

Shrinefall is made and published by Kaan İpek, working alone under the name RLD Games, from Texas in the United States. He is the controller of anything described here, and the address for every question about it is rld.ranger07@gmail.com.

RLD Games operates no server of its own. Nothing set out below lands in a database we hold, because there is no such database.

Alone, the game is silent

Choosing PLAY from the title screen starts a world that runs entirely inside the app on your handset — technically a host bound to the loopback address, which is a roundabout way of saying it talks to nobody. No sign-in happens, no service is woken and no packet goes out. You can finish the whole game in aeroplane mode.

Opening or entering a room

The first time you press HOST A ROOM or JOIN on the co-op screen, Unity Gaming Services starts up, and two things follow.

  • An anonymous player is created Unity Authentication signs you in without being given anything — no address, no password, no profile — and hands back a random player identifier, which the app keeps on the handset so the same anonymous player returns next time. It is never displayed in the game, and no record of yours is filed under it.
  • A relay carries the party's traffic Unity Relay passes encrypted packets between the handsets in the room. For as long as the room is open it necessarily sees the network address each connection comes from. The shared world itself is simulated on the host's handset, not on any machine of ours or Unity's.

While the party lasts, the other people in it receive the name you typed, the hero you picked and its level — this is what draws the lobby list and the label over each character. That information exists only in the copy of the game running on those handsets and disappears when the party breaks up.

What Unity is able to see

Any online service can see who is calling it. Unity Gaming Services therefore receives your network address, device and application identifiers, and ordinary diagnostic signals, for as long as the game is speaking to it. Unity handles that material as our processor under Unity's Game Player and App User Privacy Policy, on infrastructure that may well sit outside the country you are playing in.

Separately, Apple and Google gather crash reports and usage figures according to their own terms and whatever you have chosen in your device settings, and pass some of it back to developers in aggregate.

The App Store label, and what is behind it

No advertising SDK and no third-party analytics kit is built into Shrinefall; the project's package manifest contains neither. It does include the Unity engine's own analytics module, and the services described above are reached whenever co-op runs. The App Privacy label published on the App Store is the binding summary of all that, and it declares:

User ID
Linked to you. Used for app functionality — this is the anonymous Unity player identifier.
Other Usage Data
Linked to you. Used for analytics and app functionality.
Crash Data
Collected, not linked to you.

None of it is used to follow you into another company's app or website. That is why Shrinefall never shows an App Tracking Transparency request.

What simply stays put

Your heroes and their levels, the contents of your bag, your gold, the shrines you have brought back to light, the sliders in SETTINGS, the name you typed and how long you have played are written to one file in the app's own storage on the handset. That file is not uploaded, not synchronised and not copied to a second device; if you reinstall the game, it starts fresh.

How long each thing lasts

Save file
Until you remove the app.
Anonymous player id
Cached on the handset until you remove the app. At Unity it is a bare random string with nothing filed against it.
Name, hero, level in a party
Only while that party is running.
Relay traffic
Ends with the room. Unity's own technical logs follow Unity's retention schedule, not ours.

What you can decide

Stay offline and nothing leaves at all. Retype your name whenever you like — it reaches nobody except a party you gave a room code to. To clear everything, see erasing your data, which covers both the file on the handset and the anonymous player identifier held at Unity.

Younger players

The co-op side of Shrinefall is meant for players of thirteen and over. The game is not aimed at children below that age and does not knowingly take personal information from them. If you think a younger child has been playing co-op, write to the address above and it will be dealt with.

Why any of this is allowed

The co-op material is handled because you asked for a feature that cannot work without it, which is the performance of our agreement with you. The technical signals are handled on the strength of a legitimate interest in keeping a working and reasonably safe service running. Nothing is ever sold, rented, traded or fed to advertising. You are entitled to ask what is held about you at any point — outside a live co-op room the honest answer is nothing.
